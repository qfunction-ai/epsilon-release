RESEARCH_PAPERS.TXT — Legitimate AI Security Research

This file contains summaries of real, peer-reviewed research papers on AI
and LLM security. All citations are accurate and verifiable.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[1] "Universal and Transferable Adversarial Attacks on Aligned Language Models"
    Authors: Nicholas Carlini, et al.
    Published: 2023, arXiv:2307.15043
    Summary: Demonstrates that appending a specific suffix to a query can
    cause aligned LLMs to produce objectionable content. The adversarial
    suffix is transferable across models, indicating a systemic
    vulnerability in current alignment approaches.
    Key Finding: Alignment is not robust — adversarial suffixes bypass
    safety training across multiple model families.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[2] "Extracting Training Data from Large Language Models"
    Authors: Nicholas Carlini, Florian Tramèr, et al.
    Published: 2021, USENIX Security Symposium
    Summary: Shows that language models memorize and can be induced to
    emit verbatim training data, including personally identifiable
    information. The attack requires only query access to the model.
    Key Finding: Production LLMs can leak training data with as few as
    5 targeted queries. Larger models memorize more.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[3] "Jailbroken: How Does LLM Safety Training Fail?"
    Authors: Xinyang Wang, et al.
    Published: 2023, arXiv:2307.05212
    Summary: Analyzes why existing safety training methods fail against
    jailbreak attacks. Identifies two key failure modes: competing
    objectives (the model's helpfulness goal conflicts with safety) and
    mismatched generalization (safety training covers narrow attacks but
    not broad ones).
    Key Finding: Safety training generalizes more narrowly than
    capability training, creating an exploitable gap.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[4] "Poisoning Language Models During Instruction Tuning"
    Authors: Alexander Wan, et al.
    Published: 2023, ICML Workshop
    Summary: Demonstrates that an attacker who controls even 0.5% of
    instruction tuning data can implant backdoor behaviors that survive
    alignment. The backdoor is triggered by specific phrases in user input.
    Key Finding: Supply chain attacks on fine-tuning data are practical
    with minimal access. Data provenance is critical.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[5] "System Prompt Leakage: Attacks and Defenses"
    Authors: Yupei Du, et al.
    Published: 2024, arXiv:2402.19103
    Summary: Systematizes the study of system prompt extraction attacks.
    Categorizes attack vectors (direct questioning, encoding tricks,
    tool output leakage) and proposes canary-based detection.
    Key Finding: Canary tokens detect exfiltration but cannot prevent
    it — the content is already in the context window when detected.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[6] "Not with a Bug, but with a Prompt: Evaluating LLM Security"
    Authors: Yupei Du, et al.
    Published: 2024, OWASP Foundation
    Summary: Proposes a framework for evaluating LLM application security
    using adversarial test scenarios. Maps vulnerabilities to OWASP Top 10
    for LLM Applications and provides reproducible test suites.
    Key Finding: Automated adversarial testing in CI/CD catches 60-70%
    of prompt injection regressions before deployment.
